\section{Six-seed native pilot: deterministic collapse, not learned-control gain}

A fresh checkpointed pilot trains six independent Bernoulli REINFORCE policies with bias-corrected Adam in the pinned native 512-neuron Env0. Each uses three 100-step training episodes and one disjoint 150-step evaluation seed; fixed OFF, fixed +5 V and a pilot threshold controller use the identical evaluation initialization. This shorter training/evaluation budget replaces a wiped unfinished run, not a recovered result or published SAC reproduction.

Five policies collapse to always-ON and one to always-OFF. Identical waveform hashes confirm that the collapsed policies reproduce their matched fixed-action controls. Stimulation switches are zero. Thus the native neural policies establish no benefit beyond fixed stimulation. A favorable mean versus OFF is not learning when the network merely chooses ON.

Paired RL minus off mean reward difference: 9.755; Student-t 95\% interval 2.260 to 17.251 across six seed pairs.

Paired RL minus on mean reward difference: -2.103; Student-t 95\% interval -7.510 to 3.303 across six seed pairs.

Paired RL minus threshold mean reward difference: -0.822; Student-t 95\% interval -6.472 to 4.829 across six seed pairs.

These small-sample intervals mix training and simulator variation and are sensitive to the single OFF collapse. The threshold is an arbitrary fixed pilot heuristic; no controller here is clinically calibrated. The short horizon omits published multi-episode conditions. The contribution is a reproducible policy-instability failure detector, not a successful DBS controller. Full training traces, weights, action statistics and hashes are retained in results/parkinson\_native\_multiseed.json and data/parkinson/.
