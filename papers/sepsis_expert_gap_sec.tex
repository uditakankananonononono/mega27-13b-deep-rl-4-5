\subsection{Unsupported decision mass and model-value gap are different quantities}
A separately frozen exact performance-difference unit keeps the released expert policy unchanged. Optimal and expert initial model values are 0.875142 and 0.781845, giving a model-value gap of 0.093297. The prior expert occupancy reproduces exactly. Weighting optimal continuation shortfalls $V^*(s)-Q^*(s,a)$ by that occupancy and the expert action probability reproduces the value gap to $6.60\times10^{-12}$. Both Bellman residuals are below $10^{-10}$, and no negative action shortfall is observed.

Of this gap, 0.076812 comes from admissible estimator rows and 0.016485 from unsupported mean-filled rows. The latter is 17.6693\% of the gap, compared with the earlier 12.0998\% of expert occupancy-weighted decisions. Unsupported decision frequency and its model shortfall contribution are therefore different quantities. Most of this model-value gap is on supported rows; the presence of unsupported decisions alone does not explain the entire comparison.

This identity attributes differences inside the released arrival-reward MDP, using optimal model continuation. It is not patient regret, causal harm, preventable mortality, treatment benefit or evidence that an action is unsafe. The unsupported contribution depends on synthetic mean-filled transition rows, not observed outcomes. No expert-policy repair, support renormalization or new training is performed.
