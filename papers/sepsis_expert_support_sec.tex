\subsection{Released expert probability is not restricted to admissible estimator rows}
A frozen continuation tests the released aggregate expertPolicy against the independently pinned admissible-action metadata, without zeroing or renormalizing expert probabilities. Across 713 transient states, all expert rows are stochastic to $2.22\times10^{-16}$. Of 9,252 positive expert-policy entries, 7,014 fall outside the source admissible set; every transient state has positive probability assigned outside that set. Admissibility is the source's estimation support rule, not a clinical safety label. A positive empirical probability can coexist with insufficient support for estimating that action's transition row.

Mean unsupported probability over equally weighted states is 0.15642, versus 0.08850 under the released initial-state distribution. For the unmodified expert policy, exact transient occupancy solves $(I-P_\pi^T)d=\mu$, with residual $2.78\times10^{-17}$. It gives 9.23795 expected transient decisions per initial model case. Unsupported actions receive 0.120998 of occupancy-weighted decision mass, or 1.11777 expected decisions per initial model case. These are visits in the released mean-filled model, not reconstructed patient decisions or observed survival.

Thus the released expert policy itself uses transition rows supplied by the model's unsupported-action filling rule. A comparison against that policy can inherit mean-fill semantics on both sides. This finding does not justify removing those actions, changing treatment, calling them unsafe, or treating an admissible-only policy as clinically better. It separates empirical action probability, estimator support and in-model visitation; those are different quantities.
